Description of Experiment

We were recording our speed at 10 second intervals to estimate our distance traveled by approximating the area under the curve using left endpoint method, right endpoint method, and trapezoid method to compare it to the distance traveled according to the odometer

Method Statement: Calculating LAM, RAM, and TAM

1. Left endpoint method (LAM): For each interval, use the speed measured at the beginning of that interval. Multiply each of the first 30 speed readings by 10 seconds and add the products. Then divide by 3600 to convert seconds into hours

LAM= change in time * (v0 + v1 + ... + v29)/3600

2. Right endpoint method (RAM): For each interval, use the speed measured at the end of that interval. Multiply each of the last 30 speed readings by 10 seconds and add the products. Then divide by 3600 to convert seconds into hours

RAM= change in time * (v1 + v2 + ... + v30)/3600

3. Trapezoidal rule (TAM): Average the two endpoint speeds for each interval, multiply by the interval width, and add the results. Equivalently, count the first and last readings as half-weighted and all interior readings as full-weighted. Then divide by 3600 to convert seconds into hours

TAM= change in time * ((v0 + v30)/2 + (v1 + v2 + ... + v29))/3600

Observations Regarding the Drive

We started at a low speed. From 60 to 80 seconds, we accelerated greatly, increasing from 9 mph to 37 mph. We continued driving at higher speeds, reaching a maximum of 50 mph around 130 seconds. From 150 to 180 seconds, we decelerated greatly, coming to a complete stop. We remained stopped from approximately 180 to 200 seconds before accelerating again. From around 220 to 280 seconds, our speed fluctuated as we repeatedly sped up and slowed down. Toward the end of the drive, we slowed down further, reaching 3 mph at 300 seconds.

Conclusions, Results, and Explanations of Error

Results

All three methods produced the same estimated distance of 1.9139 miles. The actual distance recorded by the odometer was 2.1 miles, meaning our estimate was .1861 miles less than the actual distance.

Sources of error

  • Sampling: Speed was recorded only every 10 seconds. Changes between readings were not captured.
  • Rapid changes: Sudden acceleration, braking, and stops can make rectangular approximations less accurate.

Conclusion

Even though all three results produced the same estimate, they were still different from the odometer reading. Our estimate of 1.9139 miles was reasonably close to the actual distance of 2.1 miles.
